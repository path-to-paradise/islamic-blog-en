Most habits fail because they're added on top of an unchanged routine. Ramadan works differently — for one month, the whole shape of the day shifts at once: when meals happen, when sleep happens, what gets more attention and what gets less.

## Why the whole-month reset helps

Trying to build a single new habit inside an otherwise unchanged month is hard — old patterns keep pulling back. When an entire community shifts its schedule together, the new rhythm becomes the default rather than something to defend against constant friction.

## What tends to stick afterward

The fasting itself ends with the month. What often carries forward is smaller: a slightly more careful relationship with food, a habit of reading that didn't exist before, more patience in traffic. The measure of a good Ramadan isn't the month itself — it's what's still standing in the ordinary months that follow.

If last Ramadan left you with one habit still intact today, that's worth noticing. If it didn't, that's worth noticing too — and worth asking why, well before the next one arrives.
