Weeks blur together without something to break them up. Friday, with its midday gathering, acts as a natural seam — a moment set aside on purpose, in the middle of an otherwise ordinary week.

## A pause built for listening

Unlike most of the week, this is time spent mostly listening rather than doing — a sermon, a shared moment of quiet, a room full of people pausing the same errands at the same time. That shared stillness is rare enough on its own to be worth noticing.

## The value of showing up regularly

The benefit isn't really in any single Friday — it's in the rhythm of showing up most weeks, rain or shine, busy or not. A weekly anchor only works as an anchor if it's actually reliable. Skipping it occasionally isn't the concern; letting it quietly disappear from the week is.

If Friday has started feeling like just another midday meeting to squeeze around, it might be worth treating it, again, as the pause it was meant to be.
