Patience gets described like a personality trait — some people have it, some don't. That framing makes it feel fixed. Treated instead as a skill, it starts to look trainable, the same way focus or discipline are trainable.

## The three moments patience shows up

Broadly, patience tends to show up in three situations: staying consistent with something difficult, holding back from something tempting, and accepting something that can't be changed. Each one uses a slightly different muscle, and most people are stronger in one than the others.

## Training the muscle deliberately

Waiting in a slow line without reaching for a phone. Finishing a task before switching to something more interesting. Sitting with a frustrating outcome for a minute before reacting to it. None of these are dramatic, and that's the point — patience is built in small, repeated, low-stakes moments, not in the rare crisis that everyone remembers.

The next time something forces a pause — a delay, a wait, a setback — treat it as a small rep rather than an interruption.
