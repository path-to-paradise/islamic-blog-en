Seeking knowledge sounds like it requires long study sessions and finished reading lists. In practice, it usually starts much smaller — one honest question that didn't have an answer, and the decision to actually go find one.

## Curiosity as the starting point

Not every question needs a scholar or a formal class. A lot of understanding starts with noticing something unclear during a normal day — a phrase in a prayer, a practice seen but never explained — and choosing to ask rather than let it pass.

## Depth over breadth

It's tempting to collect facts the way one collects trivia — a little about everything, not much about anything. Real understanding tends to come from staying with a smaller number of questions long enough to actually resolve them, even if that means reading slowly or asking someone more than once.

Pick one thing you've always wondered about but never looked into, and spend twenty minutes on it this week. That's a reasonable start.
