When charity comes up, the image that comes to mind is usually a large gesture — a big donation, a public fundraiser. Less visible, but arguably more formative, is the small and constant version: a coin given without ceremony, a meal shared, a ride offered.

## Consistency over size

A small amount given regularly builds a habit. A large amount given once builds a memory. Both have their place, but the habit is what actually shapes character over time — it's the difference between *being generous* and *having been generous once*.

## Charity that costs nothing

Not all of it is financial. A kind word, patience with someone having a hard day, sharing useful knowledge — these count too, and they're available to everyone regardless of what's in their pocket that week.

Consider picking one small, repeatable act — even something as modest as holding a door or checking in on a neighbor — and keeping it going for a month. Small and constant beats large and rare.
