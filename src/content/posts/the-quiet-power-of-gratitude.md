It's simple to feel thankful when things go well. The harder — and more useful — habit is noticing what's still good on the days that don't go well at all.

## Naming things, out loud or on paper

A short phrase repeated often is easy to say without meaning it.

<p class="arabic">الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ</p>

Saying "alhamdulillah" (praise be to God) after both a good meal and a bad day is not about pretending the bad day was good. It's a habit of noticing — pointing at something specific and naming it, rather than letting the whole day blur into "fine" or "rough."

## A short daily practice

Try naming three specific things at the end of each day — not "family" or "health" in the abstract, but the actual moment: a phone call that went well, a meal shared, a problem that resolved itself. Specificity is what makes gratitude a practice instead of a mood.
