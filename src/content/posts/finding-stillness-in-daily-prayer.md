Most days move fast. Notifications, deadlines, errands — the list rarely shrinks. Prayer sits inside that same day, five times over, as a built-in pause rather than one more thing competing for attention.

## A pause, not an interruption

It's easy to treat prayer as something squeezed between tasks. Flipping that around — treating the tasks as what fits *around* prayer — changes the feel of the whole day. The pause stops being an interruption and becomes the anchor everything else is arranged around.

## Presence over speed

Speed is rarely the goal. A short prayer offered with full attention tends to sit with a person longer than a long one rushed through on autopilot. Slowing down enough to actually notice the words being said is often the harder — and more valuable — part.

> A small, steady habit practiced with attention will usually outlast a large one done carelessly.

Try this for a week: before starting, take one breath and let go of whatever was just happening. Notice whether the rest of the prayer feels different for it.
